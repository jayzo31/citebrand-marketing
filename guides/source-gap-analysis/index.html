How to find the websites AI chatbots cite for your competitors but not you (source gap analysis)

Updated September 2026

Short answer: Run the same buyer prompts across several AI engines, several times each. Log every URL each answer cites, and for each URL note whether your brand and each competitor appear on it. Then filter to the domains where competitor citations > 0 and your citations = 0: that list is your source gap, the pages to get onto first.

This method is called source gap analysis. It works at the level of cited sources, not visibility scores: the output is a list of domains and URLs that AI engines already trust for your category, where your competitors are named and you are not. The steps below show the prompt set, the log, and the pivot, followed by the result when we ran it on CiteBrand itself in September 2026, when CiteBrand was named in 0 of 25 AI answers.

This guide is published by CiteBrand, which offers source gap analysis as a feature. External research is linked where it is cited. The worked example uses CiteBrand's own measurement run; see the methodology note below it.

Step 1: Build the prompt set

Write the questions a buyer would actually type, in their words, not your keywords. Cover four prompt types:

  • Best-of: “What are the best tools for …?”
  • Alternatives: “What can I use instead of [competitor]?”
  • How-to: “How do I …?” for the job your product does.
  • Pricing: “How much does … cost per month for [buyer type]?”

Step 2: Run each prompt more than once

One run is not a measurement. The same prompt returns the same brand list less than 1% of the time across runs, so what matters is how often a brand or source appears across repeated samples, not its rank in a single answer (SparkToro).

Also collect answers from both the API and the consumer app. For ChatGPT, API answers and consumer-app answers share only about 24% of brands and 4% of sources (Surfer), so a log built from one of them misses most of the sources the other cites. Your log must cover both.

Step 3: The log

Record one row per cited URL per answer. Use these columns (the empty row is the template):

prompt engine date cited_url domain page type your brand on page? competitors on page
               

“Page type” is what kind of page the URL is (for example a vendor site, a third-party list, or a community thread), because each type needs a different action.

Step 4: Pivot to the gap list

Group the log by domain and count, then keep only the rows where competitor citations > 0 and your citations = 0. Sort by competitor citations, highest first.

Domain Competitor citations Your citations Prompts Engines Page type Action
             

Domains cited across many prompts and several engines are the priority: they are sources the engines return to, not one-off picks.

Worked example: we ran this on ourselves (September 2026)

We asked 5 buyer questions to 5 AI engines, for 25 answers. CiteBrand was named in 0 of the 25 answers and cited in 0. The questions were:

  1. P1 What are the best tools to track and improve my brand's visibility in ChatGPT and Perplexity answers?
  2. P2 How do I find out which websites AI chatbots cite about competitors but never mention my company?
  3. P3 How much do AI search visibility and brand monitoring platforms cost per month for a small agency?
  4. P4 AI visibility tracking tools that show what to fix versus ones that only report share of voice scores
  5. P5 What can I use instead of Profound or Peec AI for monitoring LLM brand mentions and citations?

The most-cited domains, counted as the number of the 25 answers that cite the domain at least once: otterly.ai 16, semrush.com 14, peec.ai 9, tryprofound.com 8, ahrefs.com 8, seranking.com 6, brand24.com 6, reddit.com 5, help.ahrefs.com 4, hubspot.com 4, zapier.com 4, youtube.com 4.

Why Google rankings won't show you this

In the same run, of the 195 URLs the AI answers cited, only 1 (0.5%) also ranked in Google's top 10 for the same question [SEO:serp_overlap]. Ahrefs' larger study found about 12% of URLs cited by AI assistants rank in Google's top 10 for the same query (Ahrefs). Either way, most of the pages AI engines cite are not the pages a rank tracker shows you, so a gap list has to be built from the AI answers themselves.
